Small language models are having a moment. As the field's attention
has concentrated on ever-larger systems, a parallel line of work asks
what can be achieved under tight parameter budgets through
\emph{architectural} rather than \emph{scale} innovation. Recurrent
transformers---which iterate a shared block instead of stacking distinct
layers---are among the most promising directions: they convert
parameters into effective depth, offer a natural test-time compute knob
in the loop count, and pair well with sparse mixture-of-experts layers.

This report grew from a single training run and the questions it
raised. In October 2026 the author trained a 44M-parameter recurrent
transformer (OpenMythos architecture) on TinyStories and, after
training, swept the loop count at evaluation time. The result---gains
saturate at the trained depth and slightly degrade beyond it---is a clean negative finding
with a sharp implication: test-time loop extrapolation is not free.
The ten studies collected here work outward from that finding,
covering the architecture that produced it, how such models should be
trained, and how they should be evaluated and deployed.

The report is organized as follows. Section~2 describes the reference
model and training setup once, so that later chapters need not repeat
it. Section~3 presents the empirical centerpiece, the loop-depth sweep.
Section~4 analyses the architecture (routing, attention, and
depth--width tradeoffs). Section~5 addresses training methodology
(curriculum and distillation). Section~6 covers evaluation and
deployment (test-time compute, quantization, mechanistic analysis, and
a benchmark proposal). Section~7 concludes.

the studies in this report are analytical, methodological, or position
pieces. Proposed experiments are labelled as proposed; no results are
